        Scoring, explanations and confidence

        • Nexus score: the model outputs one 0 to 100 score from five features (temperature anomaly, rainfall deficit, water-demand growth, green cover, groundwater stress). Levels: .
        • Heat score: 35 + 14 x temperature anomaly + 0.1 x (30 - green cover %), capped at 0-100.
        • Water-scarcity score: 30 x rainfall deficit + 30 x demand growth + 40 x groundwater stress, each scaled to 0-1 (deficit -20 to 60%, demand -30 to 50%, groundwater 0 to 100).
        • Flood and waterlogging score: rule-based. Storm rainfall (45 mm/day design storm in demo mode, or the live 7-day forecast maximum) x runoff coefficient (0.15 + 0.75 x impervious share) is compared with drainage capacity (70 x (1 - 0.45 x impervious share) mm/day). Score = 100 x (runoff / capacity)^1.3, capped. Imperviousness and drainage are modelled, not surveyed, and no observed flood data is loaded.
        • Action plan: each action is applied to the inputs under a stated assumption and the scores are recomputed; benefit is the resulting score reduction. Costs are indicative tiers, not quotes. Actions are ranked by urgency, then benefit per cost.
        • City Survival Twin: the districts are a stylised layout of the eight modelled areas, not a surveyed map. Every district value comes from the same model and formulas as the dashboard.
        • Community reports: leak, tanker, shortage and standing-water reports are stored as COMMUNITY data. The sample reports shown at first are labelled SAMPLE and counted separately. Consumption anomalies use a median and MAD robust z-score on readings you provide.
        • Why (explainability): each factor is reset to its seasonal baseline and the drop in score is reported as that factor's points. These are model attributions, not causal proof.
        • Confidence: 12 models trained on resampled data predict under 20 random perturbations of the inputs (240 draws). Confidence is the share of draws that land in the same risk level; the likely range is the 5th to 95th percentile. The input-uncertainty sizes are assumptions.
        • What-If: the same model re-scores the scenario. The rainfall slider is a percent change in rainfall (drier means higher deficit and higher risk).
        • 30-day projection: a scenario-based projection from the model, not a weather forecast.

        Limitations and roadmap

        • The model is trained on simulated environmental-stress data. Hold-out scores are measured against the same simulator, so they show the model learned the simulation, not real-world accuracy.
        • Live mode uses real Open-Meteo temperature, rainfall and soil moisture. Vegetation is a soil-moisture proxy, not NDVI. Water demand is anchored to press-reported Gurugram figures (not meter data) for Gurugram-area locations and modelled elsewhere; no open live water-demand feed exists for NCR.
        • Roadmap: train on historical IMD, CGWB and satellite NDVI data, validate against observed shortages, and add utility meter data.
